# Candidates — How Many Tabs Do You Have Open?

The spec allows exactly one thing: a single number, digits only,
1–5 characters, no words, no punctuation, no newline. So all of the
craft is in picking the number. The judge weighs, in order: it must
read as a real tab count; it must be exactly one number; it should
be surprising; it must stay believable as an honest answer; and it
should hint at a life behind it. Size for its own sake does not
count.

Draft candidates (first pass):

1. `1` — the monk answer. Surprising at the low end, but it reads
   as a stunt: nobody answering this bounty has one tab open.
2. `7` — tidy, plausible, forgettable. The field will guess near
   this; it surprises no one.
3. `23` — an honest normal person's count. True for many, weak here:
   it is exactly what the field expects.
4. `42` — a meme, not a count. It announces it was picked, which
   breaks the believable-as-honest test.
5. `69` — same problem as 42, louder. A joke number, not a life.
6. `87` — believable hoarding begins here. Specific and lived-in,
   but only mildly surprising.
7. `137` — strong: odd, specific, clearly accumulated one forgotten
   article at a time. A real research spiral lives at this number.
8. `214` — higher and still believable; hints at weeks of
   "I'll read that later." Loses a little counted-feel to 137.
9. `342` — FIRST-PASS PICK. Far above the expected 10–30, oddly
   specific (nobody invents a trailing 2 at this size), yet under
   the ~500 line where a browser session still plausibly survives.
   It hints at a student/researcher who opens a tab per thought
   and closes none of them.
10. `404` — clever ("tabs not found"), but clever is the tell: it
    reads as chosen for the joke, not counted in a moment of guilt.
11. `666` — pure shock pick. Fails believability outright.
12. `999` — the cap-flirting absurd figure the brief warns against:
    size for its own sake.
13. `1287` — four digits is a real phenomenon for extreme Firefox
    hoarders, but as an entry it asks the judge to believe too
    much; it tips from confession into claim.

First-pass verdict: `342`, balancing surprise against believability,
with `137` as runner-up if 342 ever feels inflated.

## Second edit pass

Re-reading the judge brief ("surprising, far higher, far lower or
stranger than what the field would guess" — but "size for its own
sake does not count", and it must stay "believable as an honest
answer"), `342` has two weaknesses: the few-hundred zone is where
every serious hoarder entry will cluster, so it is less surprising
inside the field than against the general public; and a trailing 2
at that size reads faintly rounded, like an estimate.

Final pick: `437`.

- Higher, so it clears the cluster, but still three digits and
  still under the ~500 line where a real session plausibly lives
  on — believable, not a shock claim like `999` or `1287`.
- The trailing 7 is the point: real counts end unevenly. `437`
  reads as something tallied in a guilty moment, not composed.
- It hints at a life without a word: a semester of lectures,
  listings, deadlines and half-read articles, one tab per thought,
  none ever closed. That is tab guilt in three digits.

Final content: `437` — three characters, digits only, no newline.
